Alix Earle has been promoting other companies' products to her followers for several years. [Reale Actives](https://realeactives.com/), on the other hand, takes things in a different direction. Instead of continuing to sell other companies' merchandise, Earle decided to develop her own skincare line in March 2026. The most intriguing aspect of this shift, in my opinion, is the level of control over the finished product and the message that has been established.

[Rundin and Colliander (2021)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00913367.2021.1980471) in their article in the *Journal of Advertising* propose a classification of influencers based on two dimensions: **product creation control** and **content creation control**. According to the framework, the lowest level is when an influencer performs the function of a **spokesperson**. Next, a **co-creator** is added, which involves collaboration in creating products. Finally, the top level is the **co-owner**, when a creator becomes closely connected to the brand and incorporates it into their identity. 

In contrast to the first case, the Reale Actives’ creator is much closer to the last category. In fact, [*Inc.* magazine](https://www.inc.com/ali-donaldson/alix-earle-hates-skin-care-thats-why-shes-launching-a-skin-care-brand/91276219) revealed that Earle spent two years developing the company and consulting dermatologists, while *[Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/zoyahasan/2026/03/27/alix-earle-has-mastered-marketing-now-she-takes-the-founder-seat/)* highlights her post-Accutane experience as the source of inspiration for her product, its message, and packaging. **This makes Earle much closer to the co-owner category than simply an endorser of her creation.**

However, I would not say that this is a typical example of a **sole proprietor**. Reale Actives also has a CEO, a dermatologist Dr Kiran Mian, employees, consultants and investment from Imaginary Ventures. Earle has a powerful say over the product and its communication, but the brand relies upon specialist and financial support. This demonstrates that **a creator’s control** is not always absolute.

Her influence seems to be the strongest in the content area. According to *Forbes*, prior to the product launch, Earle had featured the products in her content and intended to share the brand-building process behind the scenes. Her story regarding acne existed even before Reale Actives was created, which implies that the brand is based on a **personal experience** that her audience has witnessed firsthand.

As a consumer, I think that makes a difference in how the promotion is received. There is a big difference between seeing an influencer just toss out a product as something they like, vs. someone who has struggled with a certain issue and made something to solve it based on that personal struggle. It doesn't necessarily make the product any better, but it does make the creator-brand relationship significantly easier to **accept**. 

That is how it is different from a regular influencer collaboration. Earle does not simply gain credibility for the brand, as her experiences help create the product, and her voice guides **how people discover it**.

**Takeaway**
The Influencer Roles Typology helped me understand that the phrase “influencer marketing” could be applied to dramatically different contexts and situations. The case of Reale Actives is an excellent example of how **an influencer can move** from the media space and become part of the product, narrative, and overall business process.
