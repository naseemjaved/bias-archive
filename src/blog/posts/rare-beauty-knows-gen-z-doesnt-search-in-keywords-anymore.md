When searching for beauty products, I would not typically search something as general as “blush.” More likely, I'd search for “the best blush for oily skin.” Rare Beauty recognises this change in consumer searching behaviour and is capitalising on it. This awareness is what makes a difference in understanding **search intent** and is arguably more important than the selection of popular keywords.

**From Keywords to Questions**
Nagpal and Petersen (2021) in their article for the *Journal of Retailing*, exploring the connection between SEO and keyword selection, raise the question **of whether** the most optimal keywords are the ones that have the highest demand. The study **suggests** that successful search marketing requires close attention to the audience’s desires and needs, as satisfying them is the key to increasing visibility and gaining conversions through organic means.

**Rare Beauty is certainly a current example of such a case.** Based on the May 2026 case study, Google **reported** that the beauty brand has changed its marketing strategies due to the way Gen Z discovers products through **AI-powered Search**. Instead of targeting simple terms related to the company’s main products, the company now tries to cover more nuanced search terms referring to beauty-specific concerns that people may have.

For example, Rare Beauty **identified** questions about blushes and what type is best for the user’s skin. In my opinion, this demonstrates why search relevance is becoming increasingly complex. If somebody searches for blush and somebody else asks for the best blush for their skin type, both **may** get similar results; however, the second query tells the search engine significantly more about the user’s needs.

### **AI Changes the Search, Not the Intent**
This is where, I think, AI makes search marketing interesting. It would be easy to assume that conversational AI makes **keyword strategy** less important. I think Rare Beauty is actually a counterexample to that. As queries get longer and more specific, the need to understand the intent behind them is critical

### Rare Beauty used Search together with YouTube and Google AI to optimise its ads across the discovery moments in its campaigns. Google **reports** that the company's strategy drove a **7X return on ad spend**, based on Rare Beauty's internal data. However, this should be seen as **impressive but viewed carefully**, due to Google being both the advertising platform and the publisher of this case study.

### For marketers, the bigger lesson is therefore not simply “use AI.” AI can help find and react to a more varied set of searches, but visibility is little use if the offering does not match what the person actually wants.

**Takeaway**
Rare Beauty is set to change how Gen Z searches without undermining the fundamental importance of why they search. Search terms are becoming more casual and personalized, yet this is a signal **of** the value of consumer intent, not a diminished value. For me, the brands that will rise above in AI-powered Search will not be dictated by those appearing for the most searches, but by those **appearing for** the right ones.
